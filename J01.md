Recently, I bought a new m.2 solid state drive(SSD) for my console. It was advertised as having 1TB of storage, which I assumed meant I would have about 1TB of usable space for my games, apps, and game recordings. However, after installing it and checking the actual available storage on my console, I saw that I only had around 930GB available. This was confusing and made me somewhat mad because it felt like I didn’t get what I paid for. From a usability and user experience perspective, this highlights a problem with expectations vs. reality. As a user I expected the number on the box to match what I would see on my computer. Instead there was a big difference and no clear explanation was given during setup or on the box. This gives the user the idea that they are paying for storage that they don't get.

![User interface]()
The reason for this issue is not immediately obvious to most users as storage manufacturers calculate capacity using decimal units (where 1TB equals 1 trillion bytes) while operating systems calculate storage using binary units which results in a smaller displayed number. Because of this a drive labeled as 1TB appears smaller when viewed on a computer. Even though this is technically correct it causes communication problems . Good UX should make important information clear and understandable without requiring technical knowledge. In this case users are expected to understand a different complex measurement system which most people are not aware of.  

![User interface]()
This affects user trust. When users see less storage than expected it can feel misleading even if the company is not technically doing anything wrong which has led to some people trying to sue companies for this issue. To lessen confusion companies could present storage capacity in a more user friendly way, they could label both the advertised capacity and the expected usable capacity ( 1TB ≈ 930GB usable), and operating systems could also help by explaining the difference when a new drive is first installed.
